| Elman, *Finding Structure in Time*, Cognitive Science 14, 179–211 (1990), [published paper at UCSD](https://cogsci.ucsd.edu/~rik/courses/readings/elman90-fsit.pdf) | Printed pp. 181–185, “The Problem with Time,” “Networks with Memory,” Figure 2 and accompanying text | Feeding a previous hidden activation into the next computation makes representations depend on prior input. Copy connections and trainable context-to-hidden connections have different roles. The book uses its own ReLU recurrence and zero initial state, not Elman's sigmoid settings or experimental results. |
| Bengio, Simard, Frasconi, *Learning Long-Term Dependencies with Gradient Descent Is Difficult* (1994), [35-page author manuscript](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~bhiksha/courses/deeplearning/Fall.2016/pdfs/Bengio_94.pdf), journal DOI 10.1109/72.279181 | Manuscript §1 (pp. 2–3), §4/4.1 (pp. 9–10) | Long-range state sensitivity and gradient-based learning can conflict under the analyzed stable, noise-resistant dynamics. The attractor assumptions matter; this is not a theorem that every RNN forgets every distant input. |
| Hochreiter and Schmidhuber, *Long Short-Term Memory*, Neural Computation 9(8), 1735–1780 (1997), [32-page author manuscript](https://www.bioinf.jku.at/publications/older/2604.pdf) | §3.1–3.2, manuscript pp. 3–5; §4 memory-cell design | Products of local rates explain gradient transport; a linear cell path motivates LSTM. The original constant-error path does not justify claiming arbitrary reliable memory in a trained model. The 1991 German thesis was not independently read and is not cited as if it were. |
| Gers, Schmidhuber, Cummins, *Learning to Forget: Continual Prediction with LSTM*, accepted Neural Computation manuscript (2000), [author PDF](https://sferics.idsia.ch/pub/juergen/FgGates-NC.pdf) | Printed pp. 4–5 (PDF pages 5–6), §2.1, §3/3.1, equations (6)–(7) | A learned multiplicative forget gate replaces fixed cell retention and allows resets for continual streams. The modern forget-gate form must not all be attributed to the 1997 paper. Read these pages as rendered images because extracted text was corrupt. |
| Cho et al., *Learning Phrase Representations using RNN Encoder–Decoder for Statistical Machine Translation*, EMNLP 2014, [conference PDF](https://aclanthology.org/D14-1179.pdf) | §2.1–2.3, pp. 1725–1726, equations (1)–(8) | Separate recurrent encoder/decoder, variable source/target lengths, and reset/update gates. Equation (7) uses update gate z to retain the old state; this convention is retained throughout the book. Their phrase-pair scoring experiment is not an end-to-end measurement of our toy translator. |
| Sutskever, Vinyals, Le, *Sequence to Sequence Learning with Neural Networks*, [arXiv 1409.3215v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.3215v3), 2014-12-14 | §2, PDF p. 3, equation (1), EOS and separate-LSTM discussion | Final encoder representation conditions target generation; target length may differ; EOS permits stopping. A fixed vector is a practical compression concern, not a proof that every finite-dimensional real-valued mapping must immediately collide. Source reversal and the paper's successful long-sentence results prevent a simplistic “all Seq2Seq fails on long input” history. |
| Bahdanau, Cho, Bengio, *Neural Machine Translation by Jointly Learning to Align and Translate*, ICLR 2015 paper, [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.0473) | §3.1–3.2, PDF pp. 3–4, equations (4)–(6), Figure 1; §4 setting | A per-target weighted context can read all stored encoder annotations, reducing dependence on a single final summary. Their bidirectional encoder and learned additive alignment are not reproduced by our one-directional, dot-product fixture. We retain their previous-decoder-state → context → next-decoder-state ordering for the explanatory skeleton. |
| Luong, Pham, Manning, *Effective Approaches to Attention-based Neural Machine Translation*, EMNLP 2015, [conference PDF](https://aclanthology.org/D15-1166.pdf) | §3/3.1, pp. 1413–1414, equations (5)–(8), comparison with Bahdanau | Unscaled dot product is a declared scoring alternative; source-position normalization and a weighted context are distinct from output-vocabulary normalization. Our computation chooses that score, not the paper's entire decoder ordering. No implicit switch to scaled dot-product attention or learned Q/K/V projections. |
| Peters et al., *Deep Contextualized Word Representations*, NAACL 2018, [conference PDF](https://aclanthology.org/N18-1202.pdf) | §3.1–3.3, pp. 2228–2230, equation (1) | Character-based context-independent token representation, forward/backward language-model objectives, stacked bidirectional LSTM features, task-weighted layer mixture with a scalar scale; original downstream recipe freezes the biLM. | Devlin et al., *BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding*, NAACL 2019, [conference PDF](https://aclanthology.org/N19-1423.pdf) | §3.1, p. 4174, masked-LM motivation; Appendix A.1 Figure 3 | A brief architecture/objective distinction: ELMo's recurrent directional language models are not BERT's masked bidirectional Transformer. | Pascanu, Mikolov, Bengio, *On the Difficulty of Training Recurrent Neural Networks*, ICML 2013, [proceedings PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/pascanu13.pdf) | §2, PDF pp. 1–2, equations (3)–(5) and Figure 2 | Parameter gradients sum contributions from shared uses; state sensitivities are products of local transformations. ## Decisions made before drafting

- Keep the existing Chapter 10 vocabulary, long clue sequences, and criterion unchanged. Add a named diagnostic RNN and a fully specified window head; neither is represented as the trained Chapter 10 NNLM.
- Freeze exact fractions before implementing loops: three states 1, 1/2, 1/4; eight-edge retention 1/256; shared scalar gradient −3/4; attention weights (1/2, 1/4, 1/4) and (1/4, 1/2, 1/4).
- Use a small ReLU translation encoder to compute, rather than merely label, all source states. Its two final summaries collide exactly. Its earlier states supply Chapter 14's attention memory.
- Label the conditional decoder table and the two attention probe queries as supplied diagnostics. No learned decoder weights or translation benchmark are implied. The executable trace is a complete analysis path permitted by the chapter contract.
- Keep forward-state attenuation, backward-gradient transport, training/generation prefix mismatch, and fixed-vector compression as four separately diagnosed issues.
- No new knowledge-assistant retrieval claim or duplicated project is warranted by these chapter contracts. Existing Keyword, BoW Count, and TF-IDF shared-set baselines remain in their original project and regression checks.
